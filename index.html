Book 1

Downsy’s Speed

Page 1

This is Downsy

Downsy lives on Buttercup Farm with her mama, Clementine, and twenty-nine other cows.

She wears round glasses and a yellow bandana. Her ear tag says 321. She likes clover, puddles, and the bell song Farmer June plays at sunrise.

Downsy has Down syndrome. That means she was born with an extra chromosome. Chromosomes are tiny instructions inside every part of a body, and Downsy got one more than most cows.

Some things take Downsy longer, like long words and steep hills. Some things she does better than anyone.

Page 2

Tall Hill

Every morning the herd walks up Tall Hill to the sweetest clover. The other cows trot. Downsy walks. Her muscles are a little floppier than theirs, and her legs get tired.

Halfway up, she rests at the Big Rock.

“Hurry up, slowpoke!” called Bramble, a young bull with burrs in his tail.

Downsy lifted her chin. “I am not Slowpoke. I am Downsy. I am going my speed.”

Page 3

Stretch, Step, Stomp

Farmer June helps Downsy get stronger. Every afternoon, by the water trough, they practice.

Stretch. Step. Stomp.

Some days it is fun. Some days it is not. One day Downsy kicked the bucket over and cried.

“It’s okay to be mad,” said Mama Clementine, nuzzling her. “Mad and brave can live in the same cow.”

The next day, Downsy practiced again.

Page 4

Thirty-One Names

Downsy knows every cow on Buttercup Farm by name. All thirty-one, counting herself. Peaches. Old Moe. The twins, Dot and Dash. Bramble.

Every evening, when the herd comes down the hill, Downsy counts them into the barn, one by one.

Nobody asked her to. She just likes to know that everyone is home.

Page 5

The Fog

One evening, a thick fog rolled over Tall Hill. The cows hurried down so fast they bumped into each other.

Downsy came last, at her speed, counting. “Twenty-eight. Twenty-nine. Thirty.”

Thirty. Not thirty-one.

“Where is Bramble?”

Nobody else had noticed. They had all been going too fast.

Page 6

Downsy’s Speed

Downsy turned around and climbed back up into the fog. Stretch. Step. Stomp. Past the Big Rock, she heard a small, sad moo.

Bramble’s tail was tangled in a blackberry bush, and he was shaking.

Downsy pulled him free, very gently. Then she rang her bell so the whole farm could hear them.

“Walk with me,” she said. “We go my speed. My speed is a good speed in the fog.”

Page 7

Home

When the two of them reached the barn, the whole herd mooed at once. Farmer June wrapped Bramble in a blanket.

Downsy counted one more time. “Thirty-one.”

“I’m sorry I called you slowpoke,” said Bramble. “Can I walk with you tomorrow?”

Page 8

Sunrise at the Big Rock

The next morning, the whole herd walked up Tall Hill at Downsy’s speed. Halfway up, they all stopped at the Big Rock.

For the first time, every cow on Buttercup Farm watched the sun come up over the valley. It had been there every morning. They had always been going too fast to see it.

“This is my favorite part,” said Downsy.

The End

Some cows are fast. Some cows are slow. Downsy goes Downsy speed.

Book 2

The Big Words

Page 1

The Moo-sical

Farmer June pinned a poster to the barn. The Harvest Moo-sical was coming, and every cow on Buttercup Farm would get a line to say.

Downsy got the very first line: “Welcome, everyone, to Buttercup Farm!”

That is a lot of words. Long words are tricky for Downsy. She always knows exactly what she wants to say. Sometimes the words just come out slowly.

Page 2

Hoof Signs

When words are slow, Downsy uses hoof signs.

A wave means hello. A hoof on her heart means love. Two hooves together mean friend.

Mama Clementine knows every sign. Most of the other cows don’t. When Downsy signs to them, they just blink.

Page 3

Practice

Every day by the water trough, Downsy practiced her line, with words and signs together.

“Wel-come. Ev-ery-one.”

Some days the words got stuck, and she stomped. Bramble watched for a while. Then he asked, “Will you teach me the signs?”

So she did. Bramble was slow at signs. Downsy was very patient.

Page 4

Showtime

On the night of the Moo-sical, the barn glowed with lanterns. Hens, ducks and goats filled the seats.

Downsy walked to the middle of the stage. Every eye was on her.

She opened her mouth. Nothing came out. The barn was very, very quiet.

Page 5

Friend

Then, in the front row, Bramble lifted his hoof and made the sign for friend.

Peaches did it too. Then Old Moe. Then the whole herd. Bramble had been teaching them all week.

Downsy’s heart felt warm all the way down to her hooves.

Page 6

Welcome

Downsy waved hello. She put her hoof on her heart. Then she said it, slowly and clearly.

“Welcome, everyone, to Buttercup Farm!”

The barn shook with moos. Since that night, every cow on Buttercup Farm can talk two ways: with words, and with hooves.

The End

Some words come fast. Some words come slow. Every word counts.

Book 3

Barn School

Page 1

Barn School

Under the big oak tree, Old Moe runs Barn School for the young cows.

On the first day, Downsy packed a clover lunch. Her tummy felt fluttery.

“What if I’m the slowest?” she asked Mama.

“Then you’ll be the one who notices the most,” said Mama.

Page 2

The New Calf

That morning, a new calf arrived from a farm far away. He was very small, and he hid behind a hay bale.

Nobody knew his name. Nobody asked.

Page 3

Lessons

Old Moe taught counting with acorns. Downsy loved counting. She counted every acorn twice, just to be sure.

Letters were harder. Downsy traced a D in the mud, slowly, over and over. D for Downsy.

“Everyone learns at their own speed,” said Old Moe. “That’s how school works here.”

Page 4

Lunchtime

At lunch, the new calf sat all alone under the tree.

Downsy remembered what it felt like to be the one at the back. She walked over.

“I am Downsy. What is your name?”

“Pip,” said the new calf, very quietly.

Page 5

Pip and Downsy

Pip couldn’t count past three yet. Downsy showed him with acorns, one by one.

Pip was quick at letters. He helped Downsy make her D nice and round, and she helped him make a P.

By the end of the day, they were both a little better at something.

Page 6

Same Speed

Walking home, Downsy asked, “Want to walk together tomorrow? Same speed?”

“Same speed!” said Pip.

That night Downsy counted the herd into the barn. Thirty-two. One new name to remember.

The End

Everyone learns at their own speed.

Book 4

Downsy’s Pumpkin

Page 1

One Seed

In spring, Farmer June gave every young cow one pumpkin seed.

“Plant it, water it every single day,” she said, “and in the fall you’ll have a pumpkin.”

Downsy planted hers right next to the barn.

Page 2

The Chart

Downsy made a chart on the barn wall. Every morning after she watered her seed, she made a mark.

One mark. Two marks. Five marks make a gate.

Downsy likes doing things the same way every day. It helps her remember.

Page 3

Hot Days

Summer came, and it was hot. The other calves forgot their seeds. They were too busy splashing in the pond.

Downsy watered hers every morning. Even when it was hot. Even when her legs were tired. Even when it was boring.

Some days it was very boring.

Page 4

Fall

When the leaves turned orange, the other pumpkin patches were dry and empty.

Downsy’s vine was long and green, and on it grew one round, orange pumpkin.

On the barn wall, there were one hundred and twelve marks.

Page 5

Empty Patches

Bramble and Pip stared at their empty patches.

“We forgot,” said Pip in a small voice.

Downsy looked at her pumpkin. She looked at her friends. Then she had an idea.

Page 6

Pie for Everyone

At the Fall Festival, Farmer June baked Downsy’s pumpkin into the biggest pie Buttercup Farm had ever seen.

One pumpkin made thirty-two slices, one for every cow.

The next spring, every calf on the farm made a chart. They all watered together, every single morning.

The End

One seed. One hundred and twelve mornings. Thirty-two slices.

For grown-ups reading along

Down syndrome happens when a baby is born with an extra copy of chromosome 21. It is the most common chromosomal condition, and it is nobody’s fault.

Many kids with Down syndrome have low muscle tone, which is why Downsy tires on the hill and practices with Farmer June. Many work with physical, speech and occupational therapists, and many wear glasses.

Kids with Down syndrome learn, play, make friends, get mad and have strong opinions, like any kid. Some things take them longer. Some things they are great at.

Speech often develops more slowly, so many children with Down syndrome use signs, such as ASL or Makaton, alongside words. Downsy’s hoof signs in The Big Words come from that.

World Down Syndrome Day is March 21, written 3/21, for the three copies of chromosome 21. That is why Downsy’s ear tag says 321.

Things to talk about

  • Downsy’s Speed: Why did Bramble call Downsy “slowpoke”? What did Downsy notice that nobody else did?
  • The Big Words: How did the herd help Downsy on stage? What are some ways to say something without words?
  • Barn School: How did Downsy help Pip feel welcome? What could you do for a new kid at your school?
  • Downsy’s Pumpkin: Why did Downsy’s pumpkin grow when the others didn’t? What is something you do every day?
