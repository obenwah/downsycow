Book 1

Downsy’s Speed

Before we read

About Down syndrome

Downsy has Down syndrome. She was born with one extra chromosome, one of the tiny instructions that build a body. Some things take her longer. You can’t catch it, and it doesn’t change how much she can love, learn and laugh.

Downsy’s muscles get tired faster, so steep hills take her longer.

How to be a good friend

  • Walk at your friend’s speed sometimes.
  • Never call someone a mean name like “slowpoke.”
  • Notice what your friend is great at.
  • Waiting for a friend is a kind thing to do.

Page 1

This is Downsy

Downsy lives on Buttercup Farm with her mama, Clementine, and twenty-nine other cows.

She wears round glasses and a yellow bandana. Her ear tag says 321. She likes clover, puddles, and the bell song Farmer June plays at sunrise.

Downsy has Down syndrome. That means she was born with an extra chromosome. Chromosomes are tiny instructions inside every part of a body, and Downsy got one more than most cows.

Some things take Downsy longer, like long words and steep hills. Some things she does better than anyone.

Page 2

Tall Hill

Every morning the herd walks up Tall Hill to the sweetest clover. The other cows trot. Downsy walks. Her muscles are a little floppier than theirs, and her legs get tired.

Halfway up, she rests at the Big Rock.

“Hurry up, slowpoke!” called Bramble, a young bull with burrs in his tail.

Downsy lifted her chin. “I am not Slowpoke. I am Downsy. I am going my speed.”

Page 3

Stretch, Step, Stomp

Farmer June helps Downsy get stronger. Every afternoon, by the water trough, they practice.

Stretch. Step. Stomp.

Some days it is fun. Some days it is not. One day Downsy kicked the bucket over and cried.

“It’s okay to be mad,” said Mama Clementine, nuzzling her. “Mad and brave can live in the same cow.”

The next day, Downsy practiced again.

Page 4

Thirty-One Names

Downsy knows every cow on Buttercup Farm by name. All thirty-one, counting herself. Peaches. Old Moe. The twins, Dot and Dash. Bramble.

Every evening, when the herd comes down the hill, Downsy counts them into the barn, one by one.

Nobody asked her to. She just likes to know that everyone is home.

Page 5

The Fog

One evening, a thick fog rolled over Tall Hill. The cows hurried down so fast they bumped into each other.

Downsy came last, at her speed, counting. “Twenty-eight. Twenty-nine. Thirty.”

Thirty. Not thirty-one.

“Where is Bramble?”

Nobody else had noticed. They had all been going too fast.

Page 6

Downsy’s Speed

Downsy turned around and climbed back up into the fog. Stretch. Step. Stomp. Past the Big Rock, she heard a small, sad moo.

Bramble’s tail was tangled in a blackberry bush, and he was shaking.

Downsy pulled him free, very gently. Then she rang her bell so the whole farm could hear them.

“Walk with me,” she said. “We go my speed. My speed is a good speed in the fog.”

Page 7

Home

When the two of them reached the barn, the whole herd mooed at once. Farmer June wrapped Bramble in a blanket.

Downsy counted one more time. “Thirty-one.”

“I’m sorry I called you slowpoke,” said Bramble. “Can I walk with you tomorrow?”

Page 8

Sunrise at the Big Rock

The next morning, the whole herd walked up Tall Hill at Downsy’s speed. Halfway up, they all stopped at the Big Rock.

For the first time, every cow on Buttercup Farm watched the sun come up over the valley. It had been there every morning. They had always been going too fast to see it.

“This is my favorite part,” said Downsy.

The End

Some cows are fast. Some cows are slow. Downsy goes Downsy speed.

Book 2

The Big Words

Before we read

About Down syndrome

Downsy has Down syndrome. She was born with one extra chromosome, one of the tiny instructions that build a body. Some things take her longer. You can’t catch it, and it doesn’t change how much she can love, learn and laugh.

Talking can take Downsy longer. Lots of kids with Down syndrome use signs while their words grow.

How to be a good friend

  • Give your friend extra time to talk. Don’t rush.
  • Don’t finish their sentences for them.
  • Look at your friend and listen with your whole face.
  • Learn a few signs together, like hello, friend and thank you.

Page 1

The Moo-sical

Farmer June pinned a poster to the barn. The Harvest Moo-sical was coming, and every cow on Buttercup Farm would get a line to say.

Downsy got the very first line: “Welcome, everyone, to Buttercup Farm!”

That is a lot of words. Long words are tricky for Downsy. She always knows exactly what she wants to say. Sometimes the words just come out slowly.

Page 2

Hoof Signs

When words are slow, Downsy uses hoof signs.

A wave means hello. A hoof on her heart means love. Two hooves together mean friend.

Mama Clementine knows every sign. Most of the other cows don’t. When Downsy signs to them, they just blink.

Page 3

Practice

Every day by the water trough, Downsy practiced her line, with words and signs together.

“Wel-come. Ev-ery-one.”

Some days the words got stuck, and she stomped. Bramble watched for a while. Then he asked, “Will you teach me the signs?”

So she did. Bramble was slow at signs. Downsy was very patient.

Page 4

Showtime

On the night of the Moo-sical, the barn glowed with lanterns. Hens, ducks and goats filled the seats.

Downsy walked to the middle of the stage. Every eye was on her.

She opened her mouth. Nothing came out. The barn was very, very quiet.

Page 5

Friend

Then, in the front row, Bramble lifted his hoof and made the sign for friend.

Peaches did it too. Then Old Moe. Then the whole herd. Bramble had been teaching them all week.

Downsy’s heart felt warm all the way down to her hooves.

Page 6

Welcome

Downsy waved hello. She put her hoof on her heart. Then she said it, slowly and clearly.

“Welcome, everyone, to Buttercup Farm!”

The barn shook with moos. Since that night, every cow on Buttercup Farm can talk two ways: with words, and with hooves.

The End

Some words come fast. Some words come slow. Every word counts.

Barn School

Before we read

About Down syndrome

Downsy has Down syndrome. She was born with one extra chromosome, one of the tiny instructions that build a body. Some things take her longer. You can’t catch it, and it doesn’t change how much she can love, learn and laugh.

Everyone learns at their own speed, at school and everywhere else.

How to be a good friend

  • Say hi and ask a new friend’s name.
  • Invite someone who is sitting alone.
  • Help with what you are good at.
  • Ask your friend to help with what they are good at.

Page 1

Barn School

Under the big oak tree, Old Moe runs Barn School for the young cows.

On the first day, Downsy packed a clover lunch. Her tummy felt fluttery.

“What if I’m the slowest?” she asked Mama.

“Then you’ll be the one who notices the most,” said Mama.

Page 2

The New Calf

That morning, a new calf arrived from a farm far away. He was very small, and he hid behind a hay bale.

Nobody knew his name. Nobody asked.

Page 3

Lessons

Old Moe taught counting with acorns. Downsy loved counting. She counted every acorn twice, just to be sure.

Letters were harder. Downsy traced a D in the mud, slowly, over and over. D for Downsy.

“Everyone learns at their own speed,” said Old Moe. “That’s how school works here.”

Page 4

Lunchtime

At lunch, the new calf sat all alone under the tree.

Downsy remembered what it felt like to be the one at the back. She walked over.

“I am Downsy. What is your name?”

“Pip,” said the new calf, very quietly.

Page 5

Pip and Downsy

Pip couldn’t count past three yet. Downsy showed him with acorns, one by one.

Pip was quick at letters. He helped Downsy make her D nice and round, and she helped him make a P.

By the end of the day, they were both a little better at something.

Page 6

Same Speed

Walking home, Downsy asked, “Want to walk together tomorrow? Same speed?”

“Same speed!” said Pip.

That night Downsy counted the herd into the barn. Thirty-two. One new name to remember.

The End

Everyone learns at their own speed.

Downsy’s Pumpkin

Before we read

About Down syndrome

Downsy has Down syndrome. She was born with one extra chromosome, one of the tiny instructions that build a body. Some things take her longer. You can’t catch it, and it doesn’t change how much she can love, learn and laugh.

Doing things the same way every day helps Downsy remember. Charts and routines are great tools.

How to be a good friend

  • Cheer for small steps, not just big wins.
  • Some jobs are boring. Doing them anyway is strong.
  • Share what you have with your friends.
  • Try making a chart for something you want to practice.

Page 1

One Seed

In spring, Farmer June gave every young cow one pumpkin seed.

“Plant it, water it every single day,” she said, “and in the fall you’ll have a pumpkin.”

Downsy planted hers right next to the barn.

Page 2

The Chart

Downsy made a chart on the barn wall. Every morning after she watered her seed, she made a mark.

One mark. Two marks. Five marks make a gate.

Downsy likes doing things the same way every day. It helps her remember.

Page 3

Hot Days

Summer came, and it was hot. The other calves forgot their seeds. They were too busy splashing in the pond.

Downsy watered hers every morning. Even when it was hot. Even when her legs were tired. Even when it was boring.

Some days it was very boring.

Page 4

Fall

When the leaves turned orange, the other pumpkin patches were dry and empty.

Downsy’s vine was long and green, and on it grew one round, orange pumpkin.

On the barn wall, there were one hundred and twelve marks.

Page 5

Empty Patches

Bramble and Pip stared at their empty patches.

“We forgot,” said Pip in a small voice.

Downsy looked at her pumpkin. She looked at her friends. Then she had an idea.

Page 6

Pie for Everyone

At the Fall Festival, Farmer June baked Downsy’s pumpkin into the biggest pie Buttercup Farm had ever seen.

One pumpkin made thirty-two slices, one for every cow.

The next spring, every calf on the farm made a chart. They all watered together, every single morning.

The End

One seed. One hundred and twelve mornings. Thirty-two slices.

Downsy’s Not-Scary Halloween

Before we read

About Down syndrome

Downsy has Down syndrome. She was born with one extra chromosome, one of the tiny instructions that build a body. Some things take her longer. You can’t catch it, and it doesn’t change how much she can love, learn and laugh.

Costumes, masks and dark, noisy places can feel extra scary for some kids.

How to be a good friend

  • Ask before you jump out or surprise someone.
  • If a friend is scared, slow down and stay close.
  • Help a friend find a costume that works for them.
  • Counting steps out loud can make scary places feel smaller.

Page 1

Costume Day

Halloween was coming to Buttercup Farm, and Farmer June opened the big costume box.

Bramble picked bat wings. Pip picked a ghost sheet. Peaches already had her witch hat on.

Downsy looked in the box for a long time. She couldn’t decide.

Page 2

The Ghost Problem

Downsy tried a ghost sheet, just like Pip’s.

It slid down over her glasses. She couldn’t see a thing. She bumped into the fence, and the sheet slid off into the mud.

Downsy stomped. Then she took a big breath. Mad and brave can live in the same cow.

Page 3

Downsy’s Idea

Then Downsy remembered something round, orange and very good at growing.

“I want to be a pumpkin!”

Mama Clementine sewed an orange pumpkin vest that fit just right, with a leaf on top. Downsy could see everything.

Page 4

The Spooky Barn

On Halloween night, the herd walked to the Spooky Barn. Jack-o’-lanterns grinned along the path. Bats swooped overhead.

Inside the barn, something went creeeak.

Pip the ghost was shaking so hard his sheet rattled.

Page 5

One Step at a Time

Downsy knew what to do. She rang her bell, softly. Ding.

“Hold on to my tail,” she told Pip. “We go my speed. One step. Two steps.”

They walked through the dark barn together, slowly, counting every step. Pip stopped shaking.

Page 6

Treats!

At the end of the Spooky Barn, Farmer June was waiting with apple treats and pumpkin cookies.

“Best pumpkin ever,” said Bramble.

“Bravest ghost ever,” said Downsy. Pip grinned under his sheet.

The End

Some nights are spooky. Friends make them less spooky.

Book 6

Downsy Goes to the Grocery Store

Before we read

About Down syndrome

Downsy has Down syndrome. She was born with one extra chromosome, one of the tiny instructions that build a body. Some things take her longer. You can’t catch it, and it doesn’t change how much she can love, learn and laugh.

Big, bright, loud places can feel like a lot. A picture list helps Downsy know what to do.

How to be a good friend

  • Be patient in line. Say “take your time.”
  • If someone drops something, help pick it up.
  • Picture lists and schedules help lots of people.
  • Speak clearly and kindly when someone asks for help.

Page 1

The List

Farmer June was busy, so Mama and Downsy went to Moo-Mart in town to do the shopping.

Downsy carried the list. It had pictures next to every word, so she could read it all by herself.

Apples. Carrots. Oats. Honey.

Page 2

Moo-Mart

Moo-Mart was big. It was bright and loud, with beeps and music and squeaky carts.

Downsy’s ears felt full. She stopped and took a deep breath, the way she practiced.

Then she counted the aisles: one, two, three. Counting made the big store feel smaller.

Page 3

Where Are the Oats?

Downsy found the apples and counted out six. She found the carrots. But where were the oats?

A store helper named Gus was stacking cans. Downsy used her clearest words.

“Excuse me. Where are the oats?”

“Top shelf,” said Gus. “I’ll get them down for you.”

Page 4

Oranges Everywhere

Next to the fruit, a little calf bumped the orange pile. Oranges rolled everywhere, and the little calf started to cry.

Downsy didn’t think twice. She stopped every orange with her hoof and counted them back onto the pile.

“One, two, three… ten!”

Page 5

Checkout

At the checkout, Downsy paid with coins. Counting coins is tricky, and Downsy went slowly.

Behind her, a big bull named Boris huffed. “Hurry up!”

The cashier smiled at Downsy. “Take your time.” And she did. She got it exactly right.

Page 6

All Done

Back home, Downsy checked her list. Apples, check. Carrots, check. Oats, check. Honey, check.

“I did the shopping,” said Downsy.

“You did,” said Mama. That night, Farmer June made honey oat bars with apples on top.

The End

A list, a little help, and taking your time.

Downsy at the Doctor

Before we read

About Down syndrome

Downsy has Down syndrome. She was born with one extra chromosome, one of the tiny instructions that build a body. Some things take her longer. You can’t catch it, and it doesn’t change how much she can love, learn and laugh.

Many kids with Down syndrome have extra check-ups to keep their heart, eyes and ears healthy.

How to be a good friend

  • Talk about what will happen at the doctor before you go.
  • It’s okay to feel scared. Squeeze a hand and count.
  • Brave means doing it even when you’re scared.
  • Cheer your friend on after a hard appointment.

Page 1

Check-up Day

Today was check-up day. Downsy goes to see Dr. Rosa a few times a year, to make sure her heart, eyes and ears are all working well.

Downsy’s tummy did a flip.

“I’ll be right there with you,” said Mama.

Page 2

The Waiting Room

In the waiting room there was a fish tank, a basket of toys and a stack of books.

Bramble was there too, with a sore hoof wrapped in a bandage. “I tripped on a rock,” he said.

Downsy read him a book about fish while they waited. It helped them both.

Page 3

Thump-Thump

Dr. Rosa listened to Downsy’s heart with her stethoscope. Then she let Downsy listen too.

Thump-thump. Thump-thump.

“That’s my heart!” said Downsy. “It sounds strong,” said Dr. Rosa.

Page 4

Look and Listen

Next came the eye chart. Instead of letters, it had pictures. Downsy read them all: apple, star, moon.

Then Downsy put on big headphones. Every time she heard a beep, she lifted her hoof.

Beep. Hoof up. Beep. Hoof up. Her ears were working great.

Page 5

The Brave Part

Last came a shot. Downsy did not like shots.

She squeezed Mama’s hoof and counted out loud. “One, two, three, four, five!”

“Ow,” she said. And then it was over. Dr. Rosa gave her a pink bandage.

Page 6

Stickers

Dr. Rosa gave Downsy two star stickers, one for being brave and one for reading in the waiting room.

Downsy stuck one on her bandana. She gave the other one to Bramble.

“For your hoof,” she said. Brave doesn’t mean not scared. Brave means doing it anyway.

The End

Brave doesn’t mean not scared. Brave means doing it anyway.

Downsy’s Birthday

Before we read

About Down syndrome

Downsy has Down syndrome. She was born with one extra chromosome, one of the tiny instructions that build a body. Some things take her longer. You can’t catch it, and it doesn’t change how much she can love, learn and laugh.

Waiting is hard for lots of kids. Counting sleeps on a calendar helps.

How to be a good friend

  • Try to invite everyone, so no one feels left out.
  • Help a friend without taking over.
  • Waiting together makes the time go faster.
  • The best presents can be things you make.

Page 1

Thirty-Two Invitations

Downsy’s birthday was coming, and she wanted every cow on Buttercup Farm to come. All thirty-two of them.

So she made thirty-two invitations, one at a time, with a heart on every single one.

It took three whole afternoons. She didn’t skip anybody.

Page 2

Three More Sleeps

The party was in three sleeps. Waiting is hard.

Every night, Downsy crossed one day off the calendar on the barn wall.

“Three more sleeps. Two more sleeps. One more sleep!”

Page 3

Party Day

On party day the barn was full of balloons, and a big banner said HAPPY BIRTHDAY DOWNSY.

Everyone came. Bramble came. Pip came. Old Moe came. Even Boris came, and he only huffed once.

Every single cow wore a party hat.

Page 4

Presents

Bramble gave Downsy a new bandana, yellow with little hearts.

Pip gave her a drawing he made himself, with a big round D in the middle. D for Downsy.

“Thank you,” said Downsy, and she signed it too, hoof on her heart.

Page 5

Blow!

Farmer June brought out a clover cake with six candles.

Downsy took a big breath and blew. One candle flickered. The rest stayed lit.

She tried again. Then Bramble and Pip leaned in, and they all blew together.

Page 6

The Wish

Whoosh. All six candles went out at once, and everyone cheered.

“What did you wish for?” asked Pip.

Downsy looked around the barn at all thirty-two of her friends. “I already have it,” she said.

The End

Thirty-two invitations. Six candles. One wish that already came true.

Snow Day

Before we read

About Down syndrome

Downsy has Down syndrome. She was born with one extra chromosome, one of the tiny instructions that build a body. Some things take her longer. You can’t catch it, and it doesn’t change how much she can love, learn and laugh.

Balance can be tricky for Downsy, so ice means tiny steps. But everyone is fast at something!

How to be a good friend

  • Offer a hoof, or a hand, on slippery days.
  • Laughing together when you fall is okay. Laughing at someone is not.
  • Walk with a friend up the hard parts.
  • Everyone has something they are fast at.

Page 1

First Snow

One morning, Downsy opened the barn door and everything was white.

Snow on the fence. Snow on the hill. Snow on Bramble’s nose.

Downsy stuck out her tongue and caught a snowflake. It tasted like cold nothing. She loved it.

Page 2

Tiny Steps

The pond had turned to ice. Downsy stepped on it, and her hooves went whoosh in four directions.

She landed on her bottom and laughed so hard her bell jingled.

“Tiny steps on ice,” said Mama. So Downsy took tiny, tiny steps.

Page 3

The Snow-Cow

Downsy, Pip and Bramble built a snow-cow. It had a big round body and a big round head.

They gave it black spots from the coal bucket and a carrot for a nose.

Downsy tied her spare yellow bandana around its neck. Now it looked just like her.

Page 4

Back to Tall Hill

After lunch, the whole herd dragged their sleds up Tall Hill.

Tall Hill. The hill where Downsy is always the slowest.

She stood at the bottom and looked up for a long time. Then Pip came back down to walk up with her. Same speed.

Page 5

Whoosh!

Downsy and Pip climbed onto the big red sled together. Bramble gave them a push.

WHOOOOSH!

They flew down Tall Hill so fast the snow sprayed up like sparkles. For the first time ever, Downsy was the fastest cow on Tall Hill.

Page 6

Cocoa

Back in the barn, Farmer June made warm cocoa for everyone, with marshmallows on top.

Downsy’s nose was cold, and her heart was warm.

“Downsy speed can be slow,” she said, “and Downsy speed can be fast.” Today it was whoosh.

The End

Downsy speed can be slow. Downsy speed can be whoosh.

Downsy’s Thanksgiving

Before we read

About Down syndrome

Downsy has Down syndrome. She was born with one extra chromosome, one of the tiny instructions that build a body. Some things take her longer. You can’t catch it, and it doesn’t change how much she can love, learn and laugh.

Downsy is great at counting and remembering names. Good friends give each other jobs they are good at.

How to be a good friend

  • Ask your friend what they would like to help with.
  • Make room so everyone has a seat.
  • Say thank you to friends who wait for you.
  • Notice when someone is left out, and fix it.

Page 1

The Harvest Feast

For Thanksgiving, Farmer June planned a feast in the big barn, and every cow got a job.

Bramble carried the pies. Pip folded the napkins.

Downsy’s job was the most important one of all. She was going to set the table.

Page 2

Name Cards

Downsy made a name card for every single cow. Thirty-two cards.

She knew every name by heart. Writing them took longer, so she started each card with a big first letter, the way she learned at Barn School.

P for Pip. B for Bramble. M for Old Moe.

Page 3

Thirty-Two Places

Then Downsy set the table. One plate, one cup, one name card at every place.

She counted down one side and up the other.

“Thirty, thirty-one, thirty-two!” Every cow had a place.

Page 4

Surprise Guests

Just as the feast began, the barn door creaked open. It was Boris, with his Gran and his little cousin Benny.

Everyone counted. Thirty-two places. Thirty-four cows.

The barn got very noisy. “Where will they sit?”

Page 5

Two More Seats

Downsy counted again, just to be sure. Then she had an idea.

She pushed two hay bales up to the end of the table, one at a time, and made two more name cards. G for Gran. B for Benny.

“Room for everyone!”

Page 6

Thankful

Before they ate, everyone said what they were thankful for. Pip was thankful for Barn School. Gran was thankful for hay-bale seats.

Downsy went last. “I’m thankful for friends who wait for me.”

“We’re thankful for Downsy,” said the whole herd.

The End

Thirty-four seats. Room for everyone.

Downsy’s Christmas Lights

Before we read

About Down syndrome

Downsy has Down syndrome. She was born with one extra chromosome, one of the tiny instructions that build a body. Some things take her longer. You can’t catch it, and it doesn’t change how much she can love, learn and laugh.

Some things take Downsy longer, and that makes her really patient. Patience is a superpower.

How to be a good friend

  • Don’t give up on a hard job. Go one step at a time.
  • The best gifts come from the heart, not the store.
  • Learn your friends’ names and use them.
  • Give the jobs that need patience to friends who have lots of it.

Page 1

Decorating

It was almost Christmas, and the herd brought a big tree into the barn.

Old Moe hung the star. Pip hung the shiny balls.

Downsy opened the box of twinkle lights. They were not in a line. They were in a giant tangle.

Page 2

The Tangle

Bramble tugged on the lights. The knot got tighter. Pip tried. Tighter still.

One by one, everyone gave up and went to make cocoa.

Downsy sat down with the tangle. “One loop at a time,” she said. It took her all afternoon.

Page 3

Lights On

When Downsy was done, the lights were one long, neat line, all the way around the tree.

Farmer June turned off the barn lamps. Downsy plugged them in.

The whole tree glowed. “Ooooh,” said every cow at once.

Page 4

A Present Problem

Downsy wanted to give every cow a Christmas present. But she didn’t have any money.

“Then give something you’re good at,” said Mama.

Downsy thought about what she was good at. Counting. Remembering names. And her bell.

Page 5

The Bell Song

On Christmas Eve, Downsy went to every stall on Buttercup Farm, all thirty-four of them, counting as she went.

At each door she rang the bell song. Ding, ding, ding. And she said each cow’s name.

“Merry Christmas, Old Moe. Merry Christmas, Peaches.”

Page 6

Christmas Morning

On Christmas morning, there was a present under the tree with a big D on it. It was from Pip.

Inside was a red ribbon for her bell, with tiny gold stars.

Then the whole herd rang their bells together and sang Downsy’s bell song back to her.

The End

One loop at a time. One name at a time.

Swimming Lessons

Before we read

About Down syndrome

Downsy has Down syndrome. She was born with one extra chromosome, one of the tiny instructions that build a body. Some things take her longer. You can’t catch it, and it doesn’t change how much she can love, learn and laugh.

Downsy’s muscles get tired faster, so she plans rests. Lots of kids with Down syndrome wear glasses, and some swim in special goggles.

How to be a good friend

  • Small steps count. “Toes first” is a real start.
  • Resting and trying again is not giving up.
  • Cheer for everyone who finishes, first or last.
  • Never splash or push someone who is scared of water.

Page 1

Swim Lessons

When summer came, Coach Peaches started swim lessons at the pond.

Downsy got special goggles that work like her glasses, so she could see underwater. She also got a bright yellow float vest.

Everyone else jumped right in. Downsy did not.

Page 2

Toes First

On the first day, Downsy only put her toes in. The water was cold, and it was big.

“Toes count,” said Coach Peaches.

The next day, Downsy went in up to her knees. That counted too.

Page 3

Float Like a Leaf

On the third day, Coach Peaches taught floating. Downsy leaned back in her float vest and let the water hold her up.

She looked at the sky and counted the clouds. One, two, three.

Floating, it turned out, was Downsy’s favorite thing.

Page 4

Kick, Kick, Rest

Kicking was harder. Downsy’s legs got tired faster than everyone else’s.

So she made a plan. “Kick, kick, rest.” She kicked across to the big rock, held on and rested.

Then she did it again. Kick, kick, rest.

Page 5

The Big Swim

At the end of summer came the Big Swim, all the way across the pond.

Bramble got there first. Then Pip. Then everyone else.

Downsy was still in the middle. Kick, kick, rest. Kick, kick, rest. From the other side came a shout. “Go, Downsy!”

Page 6

All the Way Across

Downsy didn’t stop. She was slow, and she was steady, and then her hooves touched the other side.

Coach Peaches gave her a blue ribbon that said NEVER GAVE UP.

“I did it!” said Downsy, dripping and grinning.

The End

Toes first. Then all the way across.

Book 13

The Field Trip

Before we read

About Down syndrome

Downsy has Down syndrome. She was born with one extra chromosome, one of the tiny instructions that build a body. Some things take her longer. You can’t catch it, and it doesn’t change how much she can love, learn and laugh.

New places and changes can feel wobbly. A picture schedule shows Downsy what comes next.

How to be a good friend

  • Tell your friend what is going to happen next.
  • Stick with your buddy.
  • If you notice someone is missing, tell a grown-up.
  • Give your friend time on tall steps and stairs.

Page 1

Field Trip Day

Old Moe had big news. Barn School was going on a field trip to Applebrook Orchard, on a big yellow bus.

Downsy had never been on a bus. New things can feel wobbly.

So Old Moe gave her a picture schedule. Bus. Apples. Lunch. Bus. Now she knew exactly what would happen next.

Page 2

The Big Yellow Bus

The bus was huge. Its steps were tall, and its engine went rumble-rumble.

Downsy climbed the steps slowly, one hoof at a time. The driver waited. The driver was Boris.

“Take your time,” said Boris. He had learned that from Downsy, at Moo-Mart.

Page 3

Bus Buddies

Pip was Downsy’s bus buddy. They sat side by side and looked out the window.

Downsy counted the red barns they passed. One, two, three… seven red barns!

The bus bumped and rumbled, and they giggled every time.

Page 4

Applebrook Orchard

At the orchard, there were rows and rows of apple trees.

Old Moe told everyone the buddy rule: stay with your buddy, always.

Downsy and Pip filled a whole basket. Downsy counted every apple. Twenty-one.

Page 5

Where’s Pip?

After lunch, Downsy counted the class, just like she counts the herd. Eleven. Not twelve.

Pip was gone! He had followed a butterfly, far down the rows.

Downsy used her biggest, clearest voice, the one she practiced for the Moo-sical. “PIP!”

Page 6

The Ride Home

Pip came running back. “Sorry, buddy,” he said. “I’ll stay with you.”

On the ride home, Downsy checked her picture schedule. Bus. Apples. Lunch. Bus. Every box done.

She fell asleep with her head on the window. Boris drove extra slowly over the bumps.

The End

Stick with your buddy. Take your time.

Book 14

Soccer Day

Before we read

About Down syndrome

Downsy has Down syndrome. She was born with one extra chromosome, one of the tiny instructions that build a body. Some things take her longer. You can’t catch it, and it doesn’t change how much she can love, learn and laugh.

Running and dribbling can be hard for Downsy, but she is a super watcher.

How to be a good friend

  • Don’t pick teams. Count off, so no one is picked last.
  • Find the job that fits each player.
  • Pass the ball to everyone.
  • Cheer for every goal, fast or slow.

Page 1

Soccer Day

Every Saturday, the meadow turned into a soccer field. Coach Peaches brought the ball, the goals and a big bag of orange slices.

There were two teams, the Buttercups and the Clovers.

Downsy loved the green Clover shirts. She was not so sure about the running.

Page 2

Counting Off

Downsy worried about picking teams. What if she was picked last?

But Coach Peaches didn’t let anyone pick. “Count off! One, two, one, two.”

Everyone got a number. Downsy was a two. Two was Clovers.

Page 3

Downsy in Goal

Dribbling was tricky. The ball kept rolling away from Downsy’s hooves.

But Downsy was very, very good at watching. She watched the ball. She watched every player.

“You’re our goalie,” said Coach Peaches. Downsy stood in the goal and watched.

Page 4

The Save

In the big game, Bramble played for the Buttercups. He kicked the ball so hard it whistled.

Downsy watched it the whole way. She stepped left, and the ball bounced right off her green Clover shirt.

“SAVE!” shouted Coach Peaches.

Page 5

Go, Downsy!

It was the last minute. The score was tied, one to one.

The ball rolled right to Downsy. Nobody was near her. She started to run, at Downsy speed.

Everyone on both teams started to cheer. “Go, Downsy! Go, Downsy!”

Page 6

Goal!

Downsy kicked. The ball rolled. Slowly. Very slowly. Past the goalie’s hoof… and into the net.

“GOAL!”

Bramble ran over. “That was the slowest goal I’ve ever seen,” he said. “And the best.” Then both teams shared the orange slices.

The End

Slow goals count too.

For grown-ups reading along

Down syndrome happens when a baby is born with an extra copy of chromosome 21. It is the most common chromosomal condition, and it is nobody’s fault.

Many kids with Down syndrome have low muscle tone, which is why Downsy tires on the hill and practices with Farmer June. Many work with physical, speech and occupational therapists, and many wear glasses.

Kids with Down syndrome learn, play, make friends, get mad and have strong opinions, like any kid. Some things take them longer. Some things they are great at.

Regular check-ups for the heart, eyes and ears are a normal part of life for many kids with Down syndrome, which is why Downsy visits Dr. Rosa a few times a year.

Many children with Down syndrome do well with picture schedules and lists, like the ones Downsy uses on her field trip and at the grocery store. Knowing what comes next makes new places feel less wobbly.

Speech often develops more slowly, so many children with Down syndrome use signs, such as ASL or Makaton, alongside words. Downsy’s hoof signs in The Big Words come from that.

World Down Syndrome Day is March 21, written 3/21, for the three copies of chromosome 21. That is why Downsy’s ear tag says 321.

Things to talk about

  • Downsy’s Speed: Why did Bramble call Downsy “slowpoke”? What did Downsy notice that nobody else did?
  • The Big Words: How did the herd help Downsy on stage? What are some ways to say something without words?
  • Barn School: How did Downsy help Pip feel welcome? What could you do for a new kid at your school?
  • Downsy’s Pumpkin: Why did Downsy’s pumpkin grow when the others didn’t? What is something you do every day?
  • Downsy’s Not-Scary Halloween: What did Downsy do when the ghost costume didn’t work? How did she help Pip in the dark?
  • Downsy Goes to the Grocery Store: What helped Downsy when the store felt loud? What would you put on your picture list?
  • Downsy at the Doctor: What did Downsy do during the brave part? What helps you at the doctor?
  • Downsy’s Birthday: Why did Downsy invite every cow? What do you think she meant by “I already have it”?
  • Snow Day: Why was sledding down Tall Hill so special for Downsy?
  • Downsy’s Thanksgiving: How did Downsy make room for the surprise guests? What are you thankful for?
  • Downsy’s Christmas Lights: Why could Downsy fix the tangle when nobody else could? What could you give that isn’t bought in a store?
  • Swimming Lessons: Why did Coach Peaches say “toes count”? What is something you learned in small steps?
  • The Field Trip: How did the picture schedule help Downsy? How did she notice Pip was missing?
  • Soccer Day: Why did Coach Peaches have everyone count off instead of picking teams?
  • What is something you do at your own speed?
